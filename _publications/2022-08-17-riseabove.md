One of the major challenges in Alzheimer’s disease (AD) research is the filter out the risk genes and the enhancement of machine learning (ML) model performance when working with high-dimensional, low-sample-size datasets. In this study, we proposed a biologically informed feature selection strategy applied to high-dimensional DNA methylation data for AD prediction using a Hybrid (stacking)-RF model. The dataset consisted of 485,577 CpG probes, where risk genes were identified using both biological and conventional methods, followed by the construction of machine learning (ML) and deep learning (DL) models for performance comparison. To demonstrate the statistical significance of model performance, the DeLong test was applied to compare the AUC(0.83) of the two best models. Furthermore, to identify potential biomarkers, bioinformatics analyses were performed, including disease enrichment and gene–miRNA network construction, which revealed 17 miRNAs where two has strongly associated and four transcription factors (LMX1B, MECP2, MEF2C, and USF1) as potential regulators in AD.
